Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"Percept LFP Studio: offline, dependency-free analysis of implanted neurostimulator local field potentials with enforced uncertainty reporting"**, for publication as a Research Article in the *Journal of Neuroscience Methods*.

Chronic-sensing deep brain stimulators now generate longitudinal human basal ganglia electrophysiology as a by-product of routine care. The existing analysis toolboxes for these data — *perceive*, *PerceptToolbox*, *DBScope*, *py_neuromodulation*, *BRAVO* — are capable and actively maintained, but each requires an installed scientific computing stack, and some route the recording through a server. In the clinical environments where these files are actually produced, installing MATLAB or Python on a workstation is frequently impossible, and transmitting an identifiable neurophysiological recording anywhere triggers institutional review that outlasts the clinical question.

We report a tool built under three simultaneous constraints: no installation, no network traffic of any kind, and no third-party runtime dependency in the executed code. The result is a single 868 KB browser file implementing 29 analyses, including multitaper estimation with jackknife confidence intervals, full spectral parameterisation with AIC model selection, Morlet wavelet decomposition, phase-amplitude coupling, cluster-based permutation testing following Maris and Oostenveld (published in this journal in 2007), and block-bootstrap cosinor inference. Signal processing, statistics, plotting and the PDF, EDF+ and ZIP writers are original implementations.

We believe two aspects are of particular interest to this readership.

First, **the validation strategy**. With no real recordings available, we validated against synthetic signals with known generating parameters (87 pre-specified acceptance criteria, all passed) and, where closed-form results exist, against analytic identities rather than against another implementation — reasoning that an incorrect fast routine and a test written from that routine would agree with each other. Spectral density scaling recovers Parseval's identity exactly; the arbitrary-length Fourier transform matches the O(N²) definition of the DFT to 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴.

Second, **enforced reporting**. Every parameter-dependent quantity is emitted as a value–parameter pair, every available quality indicator accompanies its metric, missing samples propagate as NaN with explicit accounting rather than being imputed, and the software returns a stated reason instead of a number when the data do not support an estimate. Because these are structural properties verified by the test suite, an export lacking its parameter is not a lapse of author discipline but an impossibility. We suggest this pattern is transferable independently of the platform.

We wish to be explicit about the principal limitation, which is stated in the abstract, the discussion and the conclusions: **the tool has not been validated on real patient recordings**. The benchmark establishes that the implementations recover the parameters of signals whose parameters are known; it does not establish agreement with other toolboxes on the same file, correlation with clinical state, or cross-centre reproducibility. We consider the methodological contribution reportable on its own terms, and the validation study is our stated immediate priority, but we would rather the limitation be weighed by reviewers than discovered by them.
